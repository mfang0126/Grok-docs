## 1. Childhood (童年)

### Family instability
- Parents divorced very early (less than 100 days after marriage).
- Moved between mother, maternal grandmother (姥姥), paternal grandmother (奶奶), and father repeatedly. No stable home.
- "Everyone told me I was happy because I had food and a roof, but psychologically it was not good. I didn't know who my real parents were. As a child I felt very insecure."
- Resulting belief: "I can only rely on myself. I never depended on anyone. I didn't like talking as a child; I researched everything myself."
- "I felt the people around me would eventually be replaced, so I didn't want to depend on them."

### Two sides of the family — very different values
- Mother's side: very materialistic / status-oriented (势利眼). "If you have no value, they don't want to deal with you. You must demonstrate value." They lived hypocritically.
- Paternal grandmother (奶奶): opposite — "just live well, grow up well, eat and drink well." Father similar.
- Almost no contact with father before age ~8–10. No real fatherly love; motherly love mostly from grandmother and aunt. Everyone gave material things, but family decisions excluded the child.
- Grandmother often said things like "will you take care of your mother when you grow up?" — perceived as PUA / guilt-tripping to make the child feel obligated to provide money later.

### Early exposure to death and separation
- Witnessed grandparents' deaths (grandfather first, then grandmother a few years later), living nearby at the time.
- "I could sense that the day might come soon; when I came back that day, grandmother had passed."
- Early, deep thinking about separation, human relationships, and confusion. "I accepted too many things a child shouldn't have to accept."

### Early awareness of adult motives (but honest framing)
- "I felt early on that many things adults did had purposes, but I'm not sure if I truly understood at the time or only understood later when looking back."
- Better phrasing agreed: "As a child I just felt these things were not right / 不对劲儿." (Avoid claiming "I saw through everything" — that would sound like retrospective rationalization.)

### Primary school — teachers and bribery
- Teachers protected each other (官官相护). If you didn't pay (father refused), teacher wouldn't let you be class monitor / cadre.
- "I saw clearly that the rules were about money, not merit. I disdained paying to flatter them."
- Father had no interest in playing the relationship game, so didn't push it.

### Early technical curiosity (root of the skill)
- Started around age 8 (小学二年级) learning computers. People brought him to their homes to fix computers, swap CDs, build PCs, configure them — then treated him to meals. Parents and classmates' parents liked him.
- Continued through middle and high school: hardware, OS installs, system problems, games — people always came to him for tech problems.
- Liked electronic building blocks / parts (flashlights, radios). Computer homework was creative and strong even when teacher relationships were poor — could understand and deliver without being taught.
- "This is a through-line from childhood to now: I like solving problems for people."

### Key judgment on childhood material
- The strongest, most visual anchor is the grandparents' deaths while living nearby, plus the constant moving between homes.
- Do NOT over-claim early wisdom. Use "felt something was off" rather than "I understood adult motives."

## 2. Adolescence (少年 / 初中)

### Worst class in the city
- Middle school class was among the worst in the whole city. Teacher had a police background, transferred after too many incidents; was harsh, hit students.
- "The teacher wasn't actually that bad from today's perspective, but the biggest change for me was: no matter what happens, you must leave yourself room for a good future. Even in the worst class, you must get the graduation certificate."
- Three chaotic years; after that, felt very confused.

### Social circle
- Because he was relatively mature and chatted with everyone, he befriended kids who were "mixing in society" (在社会上混的小孩儿). Learned a lot about how the world outside school works.
- (Note: details of what he learned in that circle are NOT for the investor answer — only for context here.)

### High school entry
- Grades not great, but found a way (paid) to enter a better high school. "Anyway, found a way in."
- In better high school: realized he couldn't reach top students quickly; grades mediocre. Discovered studying abroad was possible; family had some capacity.
- Father joked: "If you can pass the English test, I'll let you go." This became the trigger.

### Technical through-line continues
- Same pattern: classmates and even primary-school friends came back to him for computer/system/game fixes. Reinforced identity as the "technical kid."

### Key judgment on adolescence material
- The teacher's line ("leave yourself room, get the certificate") is a clean, quotable influence — but keep it short.
- The social-circle details stay private; only the maturity / self-reliance angle is usable.
- The technical through-line is the bridge to Question 2 (what people come to you for).

## 3. Youth (青年 / 高中后 → 出国)

### English grind (the hardest, most visual scene)
- Father's joke → decided to actually do it. Spent ~1 year+ preparing.
- Backed 15,000 words using the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve: 1s / 3s / 5s / 1min / 3min / 5min review cycles, very disciplined.
- Plus grammar, recitation, listening practice.
- High-school second year (高二下), took IELTS cold (裸考) → scored 5.5. Family thought he was joking at first ("you're bad at studying"); he proved it.
- Family bet on him, gave money; he went abroad.
- Visual detail: took the cheapest green train (绿皮车) to another city for classes, even on Chinese New Year's Eve, in the snow. (Usable as one striking image.)

### Abroad — cost and self-reliance
- No family money to spare. Lived by borrowing money + working while studying. Got residency, then a job. "Very painful the whole way."
- Lost the opportunities his family connections could have provided domestically (jobs via relationships). Chose the harder path because he wouldn't play the relationship game.

### Work pattern and the turning point
- First real job: solved everything alone, pursued perfection. Got tired.
- Second job: manager told him — don't chase perfection on small problems; throw the question out to the team, see responses, keep thinking in parallel. He didn't do it then.
- Later became principal / vice-principal / development lead, managing several people. Realized scaling requires others' effort; 1+1 > 2.
- AI agents as modern analogy: many agents with special abilities working in parallel, then aggregating — energy multiplies.
- Conclusion reached (years later): the repeated problems came from never truly asking for help. "Many things just need one question."

### Current state (post-youth, but relevant to the arc)
- "I've completely changed it." Now has a partner for communication; he stays behind solving, producing solutions, simplifying them for the partner who enjoys talking to people.
- From a startup angle this is already changing: he no longer does what he's bad at himself.

### Key judgment on youth material
- The IELTS 5.5 cold-test scene is the strongest visual anchor of the whole answer — keep the green-train / snow / New Year's Eve image.
- The "lost opportunities by not asking for help" is the honest cost, and the manager's advice is the clean turning point.
- Current change (delegating to partner) is the arc's resolution — belongs at the end.

## Cross-cutting themes (for structuring, not for dumping into the answer)

1. **Self-reliance as operating system**: formed by instability + unreliable adults → "only trust what I verify myself."
2. **Technical through-line**: age 8 → now. People come to him for tech problems. Root of "一技之长."
3. **Cost of the habit**: missed family opportunities, exhaustion at work, slow to scale — realized late.
4. **Turning point**: manager's advice + leading a team → learned to delegate; now structurally changed via partner.
5. **Honesty check**: avoid retrospective over-claiming ("I understood everything as a child"). Use "felt something was off." Avoid victim framing; frame as decision pattern, not trauma.
